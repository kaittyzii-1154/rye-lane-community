STREET HISTORY / FIVE SHORT STORIES

How Rye Lane
became Rye Lane.

Department stores, cinema nights, independent shops and the people who make a living here. Five starting points for reading the street’s past in the places around you.

01c.1882–1949

A street built around shopping

Before today’s small shops, Rye Lane was known for department stores. Henry Holdron began trading here around 1882 and expanded into neighbouring premises. Long opening hours, regular sales and payment clubs made shopping part of everyday working life. Holdron’s closed in 1949; its name remains part of the street’s story.

Peckham Heritage: Peckham past — Holdron’s

021914–1956

An evening at the pictures

The Tower Cinema opened on Rye Lane in 1914. During the First World War, its basement also served as an air-raid shelter. The cinema closed in 1956, but part of its entrance survived. Its history is a reminder that the street was a destination for leisure as well as shopping.

Peckham Heritage: Tower cinema and Holdron’s

03AFTER 1949

Big buildings, smaller businesses

A shop closing does not end a building’s story. The Peckham History Trail records independent shops in Holdrons Arcade and a vaulted 1930s ceiling inside the former department-store complex. These reused spaces connect an earlier shopping street with different kinds of trade. The trail describes the places as they were recorded then.

Peckham History Trail (PDF): entries 2 and 3

04RESEARCH BEGAN IN 2012

People make the street

Buildings tell only part of the story. LSE’s Ordinary Streets project studied Rye Lane through its shops, migration and everyday exchanges. Researchers observed how successive migrant groups and established residents shared the same high street. It is a useful starting point for understanding the street through livelihoods and relationships, alongside its architecture.

LSE Cities: Ordinary Streets

05HERITAGE & COMMUNITY ACTION

What should the street keep?

Peckham’s heritage has also been shaped by local action. Peckham Heritage describes how the Peckham Society and Peckham Vision helped draw attention to buildings and the town centre’s character. Their work connects questions about the street’s past with decisions about its future.
