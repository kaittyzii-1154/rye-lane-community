STREET HISTORY / FIVE SHORT STORIES

How Rye Lane
became Rye Lane.

Department stores, cinema nights, conservation and creative spaces. Five moments in the changing life of Rye Lane, with sources to explore further.

c.1882–1949

Before today’s small shops, Rye Lane was known for department stores. Henry Holdron began trading here around 1882 and expanded into neighbouring premises. Long opening hours, regular sales and payment clubs made shopping part of everyday working life. Holdron’s closed in 1949; its name remains part of the street’s story.

Peckham Heritage: Peckham past — Holdron’s

1914–1956

The Tower Cinema opened on Rye Lane in 1914. During the First World War, its basement also served as an air-raid shelter. The cinema closed in 1956, but part of its entrance survived. Its history is a reminder that the street was a destination for leisure as well as shopping.

Peckham Heritage: Tower cinema and Holdron’s

1994

A former Sainsbury’s on Rye Lane became a cinema in 1994. The building later took the name Peckhamplex under new ownership in 2010. Its story shows how an everyday shopping space could become a place for shared evenings and film, adding another chapter to the street’s history of entertainment.

Peckhamplex: About the cinema

2011

On 18 October 2011, Southwark’s planning committee approved the designation of the Rye Lane Peckham Conservation Area after public consultation. The decision recognised the area’s architectural and historic interest. Its published record offers a specific moment when the street’s inherited buildings and character became part of a formal conservation framework.

Southwark Council: Conservation area decision, 18 October 2011

2017

A multi-storey car park on Rye Lane took on a different life with Peckham Levels in 2017. The converted building brought together arts, workspaces and community activity. Its story is another example of the street adapting existing spaces for new uses, while keeping everyday work and gathering at its centre.
