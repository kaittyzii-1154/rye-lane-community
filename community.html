RYE LANE / THEN & NOW

The past is
still on the street.

A history of work, shopping, entertainment and migration. Explore how people and changing uses have shaped the street, with links to the records behind each story.

19th CENTURY

From village to shopping street

Peckham’s growth was a gradual change in how people lived, travelled and traded. Historic England describes a rural settlement becoming suburban during the nineteenth century, connected to London by canal, rail, buses and trams. By the late nineteenth century, shopping was central to the town centre’s economy.

Read the area’s development history — Historic England
EARLY 20th CENTURY

Shopping was also a working life

Rye Lane’s department stores were places of work as well as places to buy things. Peckham Heritage records Holdron’s long working day, frequent sales and instalment-style “club” trading. It also preserves Doris Daniels’s recollection of starting work there aged fourteen. These details bring the history closer to the routines of staff and customers.

Read Holdron’s working and shopping history — Peckham Heritage
1920s

A street for an evening out

Rye Lane was also a place of entertainment. A Southwark local-history account records five cinemas on the street by the mid-1920s. Its view from around 1925 shows the Tower Cinema rising above the shopping street: commercial life and leisure shared the same stretch of road.

View the street photograph, dated c.1925 — Southwark / Ideal Homes
AFTER THE DEPARTMENT STORES

Old spaces, different kinds of trade

Holdron’s closed in 1949, but parts of the department-store complex remained. The Peckham History Trail describes small independent shops in Holdrons Arcade and a 1930s vaulted ceiling within the former store. The story continues through reused spaces rather than ending when a large business closes.

Read the Holdrons and Khan’s entries, page 2 — Peckham History Trail
MIGRATION & EVERYDAY CHANGE

Many journeys shape one street

The street’s history also belongs to the people who have made a living here. LSE’s Ordinary Streets project examines Rye Lane through immigration, adaptation and everyday trading, describing successive migrant groups sharing the street with established residents. Its accounts offer a way into the relationships between small shops, livelihoods and a changing neighbourhood.

Explore the people and economies of Rye Lane — LSE Ordinary Streets
HERITAGE & COMMUNITY ACTION

Who decides what is worth keeping?

Peckham Heritage describes the work of local groups, including the Peckham Society and Peckham Vision, in drawing attention to historic buildings. Conservation here is also a history of residents and organisations arguing for the value of what already exists. The conservation appraisal and management plan form part of that longer effort.

Read about local heritage work — Peckham Heritage
